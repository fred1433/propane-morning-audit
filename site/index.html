Pick a day you have already closed. In a short paid assessment, I would bring back four things:

  1. Where each figure lives. Which records Cargas, QuickBooks Online and the POS hold for billing, receivables, payments, counter sales and stock, and how each one can be read.
  2. That day reconciled, or the exact list of records that stop it from being reconciled.
  3. The rules, written down with you: posting deadline, tolerances, account mapping, voids, late and resent files.
  4. A priced pilot plan: connections, the daily run, alerts, running costs and who owns what.

Automated access to Cargas, through an API or a scheduled export, still has to be confirmed on your installation. A manual export is enough to reconcile the first day; it is not yet an automated report.

Assumptions in this sample

  • Assumed flow: Cargas holds tickets, billing, receivables and payments, and sends GL distributions and deposits to QuickBooks Online. To align with yours.
  • Export formats on the Cargas and POS side are assumed. QuickBooks records follow its public API objects (JournalEntry, Deposit).
  • Tickets are in net gallons at 60 °F from temperature-compensated meters. A quantity whose base is unknown stops its check.
  • Accrual convention. Gross margin is not calculated: the sample has no cost method.
  • No bank statement here: deposits are matched to QuickBooks, which is not a bank reconciliation.
  • Tax amounts are copied from the records as they are. Autogas and interstate tax calculation is out of scope.
  • Dealer cages run on consignment in this sample.
